The thing every metronome gets wrong

It assumes your song never changes.

You set one speed and one time signature, press start, and that is the deal for the next ten minutes.

But the riff you are practising has a bar of 7/8 in it. The show speeds up at letter C. The film cue sits at 120.7, not 120. The tune is meant to swing, not sit square.

So you stop the click and count it yourself, which is the one thing the click was supposed to fix.
Turn it upside down

A song is a list of bars,
not a single number.

Write the chart out once: sixteen bars of 4/4, then eight of 3/4, then four of 5/8, then twelve bars that speed up.

Press start once. It plays the whole thing and holds the bar line through every change.

Song mode

The join is the hard part.

4/47/83/4

Eight beats, then seven, then six. Five downbeats, not four and not six. No missing click at the join and no doubled one.

The bar counter runs straight through, so bar 23 is bar 23 whatever meter it happens to be in.

Tempo
120.7
Not 120. Not 121.

Film and library music lives on speeds that are not round numbers, and plenty of records were cut to tape at whatever the machine was doing that day.

Round it off and you drift. Over a four minute take, 0.7 BPM is most of a beat.

Note value

6/8 has six notes.
Nobody feels six.

A 6/8 bar is written as six eighth notes, but it sways in two: ONE-two-three, TWO-two-three.

Dotted quarterQuarterEighthWholeHalf

Pick the note value that matches the feel and Pendulum clicks twice a bar instead of six times. Thirteen values, from whole notes down to sixteenth triplets.

Values that cannot divide your bar evenly are greyed out with the reason, so you never end up two thirds of a click short.

Beat accents

Tap a beat to change its job.

7/8 as 3 + 2 + 2

Every dot cycles click, accent, mute. Group a seven as 3+2+2 or 2+2+3 and hear how different the same seven beats become.

A muted beat is silent but still takes its place, so the bar never gets shorter.

New in v2

Swing, on a slider.

50 straight
67 triplet
75 dotted

Eighth or sixteenth swing, anywhere from square to a hard shuffle. Most of the feel of a groove lives between the labelled points.

The beats never move. Only the notes in between shift, so dragging the slider mid-song does not touch your tempo.

You can see it before you hear it

The dots follow the feel.

Straight eighths sit square, halfway between the beats.

Turn swing on and the small dots slide late, exactly where they will sound.

Big dots are the beats and stay tappable. Small ones are the subdivisions. Eight of them, from quarters down to thirty-seconds.

Practice, not just counting

The click can disappear on purpose.

Bar patterns
One bar on, one bar off. Or two, four, eight.
Random silent bar
A percentage of bars drop out unannounced.
Silent practice
Sound off, dots still moving.

Playing along with a click is easy. Coming back in after four silent bars is what actually proves your timing.

Save it, send it

Export a song to a file and send it. A band leader writes the arrangement once and everybody else imports it. No account, no server, no sign up.
