The thing every metronome gets wrong

It assumes your song never changes.

You set one tempo and one time signature, press start, and that is the deal for the next ten minutes.

But the riff you are actually practising has a bar of 7/8 in it. The show has a tempo change at letter C. The film cue sits at 120.7, not 120.

So you stop the click and count it yourself, which is the one thing the click was supposed to fix.
Turn it upside down

A song is a list of bars,
not a single number.

Pendulum lets you write the chart out: sixteen bars of 4/4, then eight of 3/4, then four of 5/8, then a twelve bar accelerando.

Press start once. It plays the whole thing, and it holds the bar line through every change.

Song mode

The seam is the hard part.

4/47/83/4

Eight beats, then seven, then six. Five downbeats, not four and not six. No dropped beat at the join, no doubled one.

The bar counter runs straight through, so bar 23 is bar 23 whatever meter it happens to be in.

Tempo
120.7
Not 120. Not 121.

Film and library music lives on tempos that are not whole numbers, and a lot of records were cut to tape at whatever the machine was doing that day.

Round it off and you drift. Over a four minute take, 0.7 BPM is most of a beat.

Beat accents

Tap a beat to change its job.

7/8 as 3 + 2 + 2

Every dot cycles click, accent, mute. Group a 7 as 3+2+2 or 2+2+3 and hear the difference immediately, or silence beat four and leave a hole where the band hits.

Practice, not just counting

The click can disappear on purpose.

Bar patterns
One bar on, one bar off. Or two, four, eight.
Random silent bar
A percentage of bars drop out unannounced.

Playing along with a click is easy. Coming back in after four silent bars is the thing that actually shows whether your time is any good.
