## Start here: what is a metronome?

A metronome makes a steady clicking sound. Musicians play along with it so their playing
stays at an even speed and does not slowly speed up or slow down.

Three words come up over and over, so here they are in plain English.

**Beat.** One click. Like your heartbeat, or your foot tapping.

**Bar** (some people say *measure*). A small group of beats. Music is written in these
little groups. Most songs group beats in fours, so you count "one, two, three, four, one,
two, three, four" over and over. Each of those counts of four is one bar.

**Tempo** (also called **BPM**, short for **beats per minute**). How fast the clicks come.
60 BPM means one click every second, which is quite slow and walk-like. 120 BPM is twice
as fast. Big numbers mean fast, small numbers mean slow.

**Time signature.** This tells you how many beats are in each bar. It is written as one
number over another, like 4/4 or 3/4. The **top** number is the one that matters most when
you are starting out: it is how many beats you count before starting again. So 3/4 means
you count "one, two, three, one, two, three". A waltz is in 3/4. Most pop and rock is 4/4.

That is genuinely all you need to know to use this app.

## What makes Pendulum different

Nearly every metronome makes you pick one speed and one time signature. You press start,
and that is what you get until you press stop.

Real music is often not like that.

- A song might be in 4/4 for the verse, then switch to 3/4 for the chorus.
- A film score might speed up gradually across a scene.
- A marching band show changes both, several times, in a single performance.
- Some songs sit at an odd speed like 120.7, not a round number.

With an ordinary metronome you have to stop the click and count the tricky parts yourself,
which defeats the point of having a click at all.

**Pendulum lets you write out the whole song in advance.** Sixteen bars of 4/4, then eight
bars of 3/4, then twelve bars that gradually speed up. Press start once and it plays the
entire thing, changing at exactly the right moments so you never have to count it yourself.

It also does the small things carefully. When your song changes from one time signature to
another, the change lands exactly on the join with no missing or doubled click. It handles
speeds with a decimal point. And when you tell it to gradually speed up, it works out the
timing properly rather than approximating, which matters if you are lining the click up
against anything else.

## Your first two minutes

1. Open **[the app](https://rohittabs.github.io/pendulum/)** in your browser. Nothing to
   download and nothing to sign up for.
2. Press the big **START** button. You will hear a click.
3. The big number in the middle is the speed. Press **−** and **+** to change it, or drag
   the slider underneath.
4. Press **STOP** when you have had enough.

That is a normal metronome, working. Everything below is extra.

## The main screen, button by button

**The logo and the word PENDULUM** sit at the top left.

**The sun or moon button** switches between the dark colour scheme and the light one. It
shows the one you are about to switch *to*, so if you see a sun, pressing it gives you the
light theme.

**SONG** and **SETTINGS** open the two other screens.

**The big circle** in the middle pulses in time with the click, so you can follow along
with your eyes if you cannot hear it well or if you have the sound muted.

**The big number** is the tempo in BPM. You can:

- press **−** or **+** to nudge it by one
- **hold down** − or + to change it quickly
- **tap the number itself** to type an exact value
- drag the **slider** below it

**The word under the number** (Andante, Allegro, and so on) is the traditional Italian name
for that speed range. Musicians have used these for hundreds of years. It is only a label
and changes nothing about the sound. Roughly: Largo is very slow, Andante is walking pace,
Moderato is medium, Allegro is lively, Presto is fast.

**The time signature** (like 4/4) sits under that, along with the accent grouping if you
have set one.

**The row of dots** shows the beats in one bar. The dot lights up as each beat plays. See
[Beat accents](#beat-accents) for what happens when you tap them.

**TAP** lets you find a speed by ear. Tap the button along with music that is already
playing, four or five times, and Pendulum works out the tempo and sets it for you. Useful
when you know how fast something should feel but not what number that is.

**START / STOP** begins and ends the click. The spacebar does the same thing.

## Every setting explained

Open these with the **SETTINGS** button. Everything here can be changed while the click is
running, so you can hear the difference immediately.

### Time signature

How many beats in each bar.

There are twelve ready-made choices: **2/2, 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 6/4, 3/8, 5/8, 6/8, 7/8,
9/8** and **12/8**. If you are not sure, 4/4 is the right answer for most music.

Choose **Custom** to type any top number from 1 to 32, over a bottom number of 1, 2, 4, 8,
16 or 32.

**What the bottom number means.** The top number is how many beats you count. The bottom
number says what kind of note counts as one beat. It is written as a fraction: 4 means
quarter notes, 8 means eighth notes, 2 means half notes. If that sounds confusing, ignore
it for now. Just pick the time signature your sheet music shows, or leave it on 4/4.

### Note value

**This is the setting most people can safely ignore.** If you leave it alone, everything
works normally. But it is genuinely useful once you meet certain kinds of music, so here
is what it does.

The note value answers the question: *when the app says 120 BPM, 120 of what?*

Normally the answer is quarter notes, which is the ordinary everyday beat. But sometimes
you want something else. The classic case is 6/8 time, used in a lot of jigs, ballads and
film music. Written down, a 6/8 bar has six eighth notes in it. But nobody *feels* six
beats in a 6/8 bar. It feels like **two** big swaying pulses, each made of three little
notes: "ONE-two-three TWO-two-three".

So in 6/8 you can set the note value to **Dotted quarter**, and Pendulum clicks twice per
bar instead of six times, which is how the music actually feels.

There are thirteen choices, from the very long to the very short:

Whole, Dotted half, Half, Dotted quarter, Quarter, Quarter triplet, Dotted eighth, Eighth,
Eighth triplet, Dotted sixteenth, Sixteenth, Sixteenth triplet, Thirty-second.

A "dotted" note is one and a half times the length of the plain one. A "triplet" note is
one third of the way through squeezing three notes into the space of two.

**Some combinations are greyed out, and that is correct.** A note value only works if it
divides the bar evenly. You cannot fit a whole number of dotted quarters into a 4/4 bar,
because it comes to two and two thirds, and there is no such thing as two thirds of a
click. Pendulum greys those out and tells you why if you hover over them. Out of every
possible combination, roughly a third are impossible for this reason, so seeing greyed
options is normal and not a bug.

**The speed name adjusts too.** If you set the note value to Eighth and the tempo to 120,
that is 120 eighth notes per minute, which is only 60 quarter notes per minute. So the
label reads Larghetto, a slow marking, rather than Allegro. This is correct and is what a
musician would expect.

Next to the picker Pendulum always tells you the result in plain words, for example
"6/8 at dotted quarter = 2 pulses per bar", so you never have to work it out yourself.

### Beat accents

Usually the first beat of a bar is louder than the others. That louder beat is called the
**downbeat**, and it is how you keep track of where you are in the bar.

In Pendulum, **tap any dot in the row of dots** to change what that beat does. Each tap
moves it to the next of three states:

1. **Click**: a normal beat
2. **Accent**: a louder, higher beat
3. **Mute**: silent, but the time is still counted

Tap again and it goes back to a normal click.

**Muting is not the same as removing.** A muted beat still takes up its space in the bar.
If you mute beat two of a four-beat bar, beat three still arrives at exactly the same
moment it always did. There is simply a gap where beat two used to be. This is very useful
for practising a part where the band drops out.

**Accent grouping.** Under the dots you will see something like `3+2+2`. This is shorthand
for where the accents fall. A bar of seven beats accented as 3+2+2 means the accents are on
beats 1, 4 and 6. You can tap the dots and watch the shorthand update, or type the
shorthand and watch the dots update. There are also one-tap buttons for the common
groupings of whatever bar length you are using.

This matters a great deal in music with odd bar lengths. A bar of seven grouped 3+2+2 feels
completely different from the same seven grouped 2+2+3, even though the speed is identical.

### Subdivisions

A subdivision fills in extra, quieter clicks between the main beats. This helps you place
notes accurately in the gaps.

There are eight choices:

| Setting | Extra clicks per beat | Count it as |
|---|---|---|
| Quarter | none, just the beat | "1, 2, 3, 4" |
| Eighth | 1 | "1 and 2 and" |
| Triplet 8ths | 2 | "1 trip-let 2 trip-let" |
| 16th | 3 | "1 e and a 2 e and a" |
| Quintuplet | 4 | five evenly per beat |
| Triplet 16ths | 5 | six evenly per beat |
| Septuplet | 6 | seven evenly per beat |
| 32nd | 7 | eight evenly per beat |

The subdivision clicks are deliberately quieter and duller than the main beats so you can
still tell which is which.

**The dots follow the subdivision.** Turn on eighth notes in a four-beat bar and the row
shows eight dots: four large ones for the beats and four small ones in between. Only the
large dots respond to tapping. If the total would be more than 32 dots, they get too small
to see, so Pendulum hides the small ones and tells you it has done so.

### Swing

Swing is that relaxed, loping, jazzy feel. Instead of the in-between notes sitting exactly
halfway, the first one is held longer and the second arrives late. Think of the difference
between "da-da-da-da" and "daa-da daa-da".

**Turn it on with three buttons:** Off, 8th, or 16th. This chooses which notes get swung.
8th is by far the more common and is what almost all jazz, blues and shuffle uses.

**Then use the slider** to set how much. It runs from 50 to 75, with three labelled points:

- **50: Straight.** No swing at all, identical to turning it off.
- **67: Triplet.** The classic jazz swing. The first note is twice as long as the second.
- **75: Dotted.** A harder, sharper shuffle. The first note is three times as long.

Anything in between works too. Real players sit at different points depending on the tune
and the tempo, and a lot of the feel of a groove lives in that choice.

**Two useful things to know.** First, choosing a swing mode automatically sets the
subdivision to match, because swing needs those in-between notes to exist before it can
move them. Second, **swing never changes your tempo**. Only the in-between notes shift; the
main beats stay locked exactly where they were. You can drag the slider while the click is
running and the bar length will not budge.

You can also *see* the swing. The small dots slide along the row to sit where they will
actually sound, so at 67 they sit two thirds of the way across rather than in the middle.

### Bar pattern

This makes the click disappear for whole bars at a time, which is the single best way to
test whether your sense of time is really steady.

- **Always on**: normal, the click never stops.
- **1 on / 1 off**: plays a bar, then goes silent for a bar, then repeats.
- **2 on / 2 off**, **3 on / 3 off**, **4 on / 4 off**, **8 on / 8 off**: the same idea
  with longer stretches.
- **Random silent bar**: bars drop out unpredictably. You set the chance as a percentage.
  30% is a good starting point.
- **Custom**: type your own number of bars on and off.

During the silent bars the dots keep moving, so you can still see where you are if you
want. Keep playing, and when the click returns you find out immediately whether you drifted.

Playing along with a click is easy. Coming back in perfectly after four silent bars is the
thing that actually proves your timing.

### Polyrhythm

A polyrhythm is two different speeds running at the same time, evenly spaced, both fitting
into the same bar. "3 against 2" means one part plays three evenly spaced notes while the
other plays two, and they line up again at the start.

Pendulum has seven ready-made ones: **3:2, 4:3, 5:4, 3:4, 5:3, 7:8** and **2:3**. You can
also type your own, using any two numbers from 2 to 16.

The second rhythm gets **its own sound and its own volume**, which matters enormously. If
both parts used the same click you would hear one confusing muddle instead of two separate
things. Give the main beat a wood block and the polyrhythm a shaker and suddenly you can
hear both parts clearly.

The second row of dots below the main one shows the polyrhythm.

### Sounds and volume

There are fourteen click sounds: **Classic, Wood Block, Hi-Hat, Cowbell, Digital, Soft,
Rimshot, Kick, Claves, Bell, Shaker, Stick Click, Snare** and **Tom**.

They are all generated by the app itself rather than being recorded audio files, which is
why the whole thing stays small and works with no internet.

You set the sound and the volume **separately for the main beat and for the polyrhythm**.
Tapping a sound plays it straight away so you can compare without guessing.

There is also a **master volume**, and a **silent practice** switch that turns the sound off
completely while leaving the dots and the pulsing circle running. Silent practice is good
for late at night, or for training yourself to feel the beat rather than lean on hearing it.

### Count-in

Gives you a bar or more of clicks before the real thing begins, so you are not caught out
by the very first beat. Choose **Off, 1, 2, 4** or **8** bars. A countdown appears over the
circle so you can see how long is left.

### Appearance and comfort

- **Light and dark themes**, remembered between visits.
- **Reduce motion**: keeps the colour flash but stops things growing and shrinking. Good
  if animation bothers you or makes you feel unwell.
- **Keep screen awake**: stops your phone locking itself while the metronome is running.
  This only appears if your device supports it.
- Full keyboard control and screen-reader labels throughout.

## Song mode

This is the reason Pendulum exists. Press **SONG** at the top of the screen.

A song is a list of **sections**. Each section is a chunk of music that keeps the same
settings, and for each one you set:

- a **name**, so you can label it Verse, Chorus, Bridge, or Letter C
- how many **bars** it lasts
- its **time signature**
- its **note value**
- its **accent pattern**, using the same tappable dots as the main screen
- its **tempo**
- an optional **target tempo**, which makes it speed up or slow down across the section
- its **subdivision**
- how many times it **repeats**
- its own **bar pattern**

You can drag sections to reorder them, duplicate one, or delete it. The whole song can loop
a set number of times or forever.

**While it is playing** you get a live readout: which section you are in, which bar of that
section, the overall bar number, the current time signature, the current tempo, and how
much time has passed and is left.

**The bar map** across the top shows one small tick per bar, coloured by section, with a
marker showing where you are. Tap any tick to jump straight to that bar. Very handy when
you want to drill one tricky corner without playing the whole piece.

**The estimated total time** updates as you edit, both for each section and for the whole
song.

### The speed trainer

Press the stepped ramp button and fill in four boxes: **start tempo, end tempo, how many
steps, and how long in total**. Pendulum builds all the sections for you.

Ask for 90 to 180 over ten minutes in twenty steps and you get twenty sections, each a
little faster than the last, ending exactly on 180. This is the standard way to build speed
on a difficult passage without your hands tensing up.

If you would rather have a smooth change than steps, put a target tempo on a single section
instead and the speed moves a fraction on every single beat.

### Saving and sharing

- **Save, rename, duplicate and delete** as many songs as you like.
- **Export** writes the song to a small file on your device.
- **Import** reads one back in.

Because a song is just a file, a band leader or teacher can write out a tricky arrangement
once and send it to everybody else. There is no account and no server involved.

### Ready-made examples

Three examples are built in so the feature is not a blank page the first time you open it:

- **Marching show**: 16 bars of 4/4 at 144, 8 bars of 3/4 at 120, 4 bars of 5/8, 12 bars
  speeding up from 120 to 168, then 8 bars of 6/8.
- **Odd-time riff**: bars of 7/8 grouped 3+2+2, mixed with 4/4 and 5/4.
- **Speed trainer 80 to 150**: a ready-built ramp.

Open one, play it, then change it. It is the fastest way to see what song mode does.

## Presets

A preset saves everything on the main screen under a name you choose: tempo, time
signature, note value, accents, subdivision, swing, bar pattern, polyrhythm, both sounds,
both volumes and the count-in.

Use these for the settings you return to often, like your daily warm-up. Presets are for a
single fixed setup; song mode is for something that changes as it goes. There is a button
to turn any preset into a song section if you decide you want to build a chart from it.

## Installing it like a real app

Pendulum can sit on your home screen with its own icon and open full screen with no browser
bars. There is no app store, no download and no permissions.

**On iPhone or iPad:** open it in Safari, tap the Share button (the square with the arrow
pointing up), scroll down, and tap **Add to Home Screen**.

**On Android:** Chrome will usually offer to install it by itself. If it does not, open the
three-dot menu and choose **Install app** or **Add to Home screen**.

**On a computer:** look for a small install icon in the address bar in Chrome or Edge.

Once installed it opens with **no internet connection at all**. Useful in a rehearsal room
with thick walls, in an orchestra pit, on the underground, or on a plane.

## Your privacy

There is no server. Nothing you do is sent anywhere.

- No account, no sign up, no email address.
- No tracking, no analytics, no cookies for advertising.
- No uploads. Your songs and presets are stored by your own browser, on your own device.
- No ads.

The entire app is a single HTML file with the fonts and icons built into it.

The one thing worth knowing: because your songs live in your browser's own storage, they
belong to that browser on that device. They do not follow you to another phone, and
clearing your browser data will remove them. If a song matters to you, use **Export** to
save it as a file.

## Practice recipes

Concrete things to try, and why.

**You keep rushing.** Set a comfortable tempo and turn on **1 on / 1 off**. Play through the
silent bars. If the click comes back early against you, you are rushing.

**A fast passage keeps falling apart.** Use the **speed trainer**. Start well below the
speed that breaks down, end slightly above your target, and use fifteen or twenty steps over
ten minutes. Faster than you can play cleanly teaches your hands to tense.

**You cannot feel a 7/8 bar.** Set the time signature to 7/8, tap the dots to accent 1, 4
and 6, and check the shorthand reads `3+2+2`. Play it until it feels like a normal bar, then
try `2+2+3` and hear how different the same seven beats become.

**Your swing feels stiff.** Turn on **8th swing** and put the slider at 67. Play along. Then
move it to 60 and to 72 and notice how much the groove changes. Most players settle
somewhere that is not exactly on a named point.

**A 6/8 tune feels like a march.** Set the note value to **Dotted quarter**. Two pulses per
bar instead of six will make it sway instead of stomp.

**You want to hear where the beat really is.** Turn on **silent practice** so only the dots
move. Play a phrase, then turn the sound back on and see whether you finished where you
thought you did.

**You are learning a piece with a meter change.** Build it in **song mode** with the real
bar counts. Then use the bar map to jump straight to the join and drill only that.

## Common questions

**Do I need an account?**
No. There is nothing to sign up for.

**Does it cost anything?**
No, and there is no paid tier.

**Does it work without internet?**
Yes, once you have opened it once. Add it to your home screen and it works completely
offline afterwards.

**Why is my phone silent when the app looks like it is playing?**
On iPhone, check the small switch on the side of the phone. If it is set to silent,
some audio is muted. Pendulum works around this, but if you still hear nothing, turn the
switch off, turn the volume up, and check the master volume inside the app.

**Why can I not choose some note values?**
Because they do not divide your bar evenly. See [Note value](#note-value). Hover over a
greyed option and it will tell you why.

**Where did my songs go?**
They are stored by the browser you used. Using a different browser or device, or clearing
your browsing data, will not show them. Export important songs to a file.

**Can I share a song with my band?**
Yes. Export it, send the file, and they import it.

**Why does 120 sometimes say Allegro and sometimes Larghetto?**
Because the speed name follows the actual musical speed, not the raw number. 120 eighth
notes per minute is only 60 quarter notes per minute, which is a slow marking. This is
correct.

**Is my playing recorded or listened to?**
No. Pendulum never asks for microphone access and has no server to send anything to.

**Does swing change my tempo?**
No. Only the in-between notes move. The main beats stay exactly where they were.

## If something goes wrong

**No sound.** Check your device volume, the silent switch on iPhone, the master volume in
Settings, and whether silent practice is switched on. Some browsers also need one tap on
the page before they allow any sound, so press START rather than expecting sound
immediately.

**The click stutters.** Close some other tabs. Very high tempos combined with very fine
subdivisions ask a lot of a slow device.

**It looks out of date after an update.** Close every tab that has Pendulum open, then
reopen it. The offline copy refreshes on the next fresh load.

**The screen keeps turning off.** Turn on **Keep screen awake** in Settings. If you do not
see it, your browser does not support it, and you will need to lengthen your screen timeout
in your device settings.

**It will not install.** Installing needs a proper `https://` address. Use the link at the
top of this page rather than a downloaded copy.

## For developers

Everything is static. Nothing is built, bundled or compiled at deploy time.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/rohittabs/pendulum.git
cd pendulum
python3 -m http.server 8080
```

Then open `http://localhost:8080`.

Opening `index.html` straight from disk mostly works, but service workers only register

### How the timing works

The click is scheduled using the Web Audio lookahead pattern rather than `setInterval`. A
Web Worker ticks every 25 ms and the scheduler queues every event falling inside the next
150 ms using exact `AudioContext` timestamps.

Two reasons this matters. Browsers throttle main-thread timers to roughly once a second once
a tab goes to the background, so a timer-driven metronome falls apart the moment you switch
apps. And `setInterval` drift accumulates, so a naive implementation is measurably late
after a few minutes.

Some specific decisions:

- **Changing a setting mid-playback** applies from the first event that has not been
  scheduled yet, rather than rebuilding from the beginning. Rebuilding produces duplicate
  clicks and backwards jumps at the boundary.
- **Tempo ramps are integrated beat by beat**, not averaged across the section. Averaging
  gives the wrong total duration, and it fails silently.
- **Swing shifts only subdivisions.** Pulses stay locked to the grid, so bar length is
  identical at every swing amount.
- **Random silent bars use a seeded generator**, so the audio and the visuals always agree
  about which bars are silent.
- **Muted beats still occupy their slot**, so a mute never shortens a bar.
- **Tap tempo uses the median** of recent taps, so one sloppy tap does not move the tempo.
- **Note values allow a 1e-9 tolerance** when checking whether they divide a bar evenly,
  because one third and one sixth are not exact in binary.

Thirty-second notes at 300 BPM are 25 ms apart and play evenly.
